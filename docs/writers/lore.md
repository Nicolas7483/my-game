# Lore Bible: The World of Candlemere

## The Pitch
Candlemere is a gentle world of tide-towns, lantern festivals and fat sheep. Its light is not
sunlight alone. It is made of kept promises. And lately, quietly, the promises are going dark.

## The Magic: Glims
- **What they are:** every kept promise leaves behind a glim, a warm mote of light the size of a
  firefly. Keep a promise to feed the cat, and a tiny glim drifts up from the empty bowl.
- **Big promises make big light.** A wedding vow can light a whole street.
- **Lanterns hold them.** Folk catch glims in glass lanterns called **wicks** and use them to power
  ferries, looms, mill wheels and bridges. A healthy village glows gold after dusk.
- **Broken promises leave Dimspots:** grey patches where color drains out of the world. Flowers turn
  ash-pale, songs go flat, and people standing inside one forget small things (names, recipes).
- **Gameplay read:** glowing means cared for. Grey means something was let go.

## Core Mystery: The Fading Wicks
Puddlewick's lanterns are dimming one by one, and no promise has been broken. Each time a wick
goes out, a villager loses a memory of someone they love. The glims are not dying. **They are
being taken.** Across the river, the old bridge collapsed the same night the first wick failed,
and a trail of glims was seen drifting upstream, toward something that hums.

Layers for later chapters:
- Someone is harvesting kept promises, not broken ones.
- The bridge was built on the oldest promise in the village, and it has been un-made.
- The village founder's promise may never have been kept at all.

## The Villain: The Hushwarden (origin only)
- Once the most gifted Wickwarden alive, entrusted with the **First Lantern**, which holds the glim
  of the promise that made the world: "the light will always come back."
- Watched a dear one wait forever on a promise never kept. That Dimspot never healed.
- Concluded that promises are cruelty on a delay. Every kept promise is also a chance to break one.
- **Goal:** gather every glim in Candlemere into the First Lantern, sealing the world in one
  perfect, unchanging light. No new promises, so no new heartbreak. Everyone safe, forever.
- **Why it is sympathetic:** they want no one to ever wait at a window again.
- **Why it is wrong:** a promise that cannot be broken is not a promise, it is a cage. Taking the
  glims takes the memories they were made from. A world without risk is a world without love.

## Factions
- **The Wickwardens:** a lantern-keepers' order in patched blue coats. Once heroic, now mostly
  paperwork. Their ledgers track every great vow. They are hiding that the First Lantern is missing.
- **The Tidehands:** river and sea traders who seal every deal with a handshake over water. Loud,
  generous, practical. They trade glims like coin.
- **The Hush:** quiet pilgrims in grey veils who have taken a vow to make no vows. They believe
  the Hushwarden is a savior. Many are grieving people offered relief. Not all are cruel.

## Regions (future chapters)
- **The Brinelip Coast:** tide-towns where river meets sea. *Mood:* cozy, salty, bright.
  *Landmark:* the Gullstair, a cliff path of 1,000 lantern hooks.
- **Thistledown Reach:** rolling meadows of sheep and wind-songs. *Mood:* open, lazy, nostalgic.
  *Landmark:* the Oathstones, a ring where farmers swear harvest vows.
- **The Mothwood:** a deep forest where old glims go to sleep in the moss. *Mood:* hushed, eerie,
  tender. *Landmark:* the Nodding Tree, whose lantern-fruit blink awake at night.
- **Saltglass Flats:** a white desert of glass left by a lightning-struck sea. *Mood:* lonely,
  dazzling. *Landmark:* the Mirror Caravanserai, an inn built from giant shards.
- **The Belfry Isles:** floating rock islands linked by bell-rope bridges. *Mood:* airy, whimsical,
  a bit dangerous. *Landmark:* the Great Bell that rings only when a vow is spoken.
- **The Greywake:** the widest Dimspot in the world, where color stopped. *Mood:* silent, sad,
  final. *Landmark:* the Empty Window, a lone house where someone once waited.

## Flavor Facts (townsfolk, books, signs)
1. A promise made on a full moon glows twice as bright. Or so Grandma says.
2. Never blow out a wick. You catch the glim in your palm and let it rest.
3. Tidehands shake on a deal with one hand in the river. The water remembers.
4. Moths love glims. A moth on your lantern means someone kept their word to you.
5. If you forget a friend's name, check for grey grass. You may be standing in a Dimspot.
6. The Wickwardens keep a ledger of great vows. Nobody has seen the first page.
7. Children promise to come home by dusk. That is why dusk is so golden here.
8. Hush pilgrims never say "I will." They say "perhaps," and they smile when they do.
9. Plumcake is traditional at a promise feast. Nobody remembers why. It is just good.
10. A kept promise to yourself makes the smallest glim. It is also the hardest to catch.

## The Village: Puddlewick
- **Where:** a crooked little harbor where the Tamble River spills into the sea. Stilted houses,
  gulls on every roof, a wick on every door.
- **Founding:** 200 years ago, a ferrywoman named **Old Tamsy** promised to carry anyone across
  the Tamble, day or night, for free. Her kept promise grew so bright that people settled around
  its glow. When she grew too old to row, the village built **Tamsy's Span**, a bridge powered by
  her very first glim, so the promise would outlive her.
- **What it makes:** hand-blown lantern glass, "wick-floats" (glowing fishing buoys), and plum
  cakes. Trades glass and smoked fish upriver.
- **The ache:** Tamsy's Span now lies broken in the meadow river, and the lanterns on the
  hill are going out, one family at a time.
- **Nearby area:** the Tamblemeadow, a meadow at the forest edge. River, clover, lost sheep, the
  broken Span, and the first Dimspot anyone in Puddlewick has ever seen.

### Village Landmarks (examine lines)
- **The Sleepy Gull Inn**
  - "A warm inn smelling of chowder and wet boots. The fire crackles like it's gossiping."
  - "A board by the door: ROOMS 10 COPPER. PROMISES ACCEPTED IF KEPT BY BREAKFAST."
- **Tamsy's Well**
  - "Old Tamsy's well. Coins at the bottom glow faintly, each one a wish she carried."
  - "You lean in and hear your echo say your name. It sounds a little farther away."
- **The Wick Shrine**
  - "A small shrine of stacked lanterns. One flame for every family in Puddlewick."
  - "Three lanterns are dark. Someone has left a plum cake beside each one anyway."
- **Pell's Glass Stall**
  - "Bottles, bells and tiny glass sheep. A sign reads: YOU BREAK IT, YOU PROMISE TO FIX IT."
  - "A sunlit float spins on a string, casting little gold fish across the cobbles."
- **The Honeywheel Mill**
  - "The mill wheel turns on glim-light, creaking a slow tune. Flour dusts every beam."
  - "The wheel stutters, stops, then groans back to life. The miller pretends not to notice."
- **Ferry Dock**
  - "Tamsy's old ferry boat, painted every spring. Nobody has rowed it in years."
  - "Rope marks are worn deep in the post. Someone tied up here thousands of times."
- **The Wickwarden Post**
  - "A tiny blue office with a huge ledger. The warden inside is asleep on page 400."
  - "A notice pinned crooked: DIMMING IS NATURAL. DO NOT PANIC. DO NOT ASK QUESTIONS."
- **Hilltop Lantern Row**
  - "Every house on the hill hangs a wick. From here you can see the whole bay glow."
  - "One door has no lantern, just an empty hook. The paint around it has gone grey."
